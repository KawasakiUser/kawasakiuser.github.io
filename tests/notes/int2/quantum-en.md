
## Free-particle quantum mechanics

For one nonrelativistic particle of mass $m$ in one space dimension with zero potential, use the phase convention $\exp[i(kx-\omega t)]$. Substitution into the free Schrödinger equation gives the dispersion below.

$$
\begin{aligned}
i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\partial_x^2\psi, \\
\psi(x,t) &= A\exp[i(kx-\omega t)], \\
\hbar\omega &= \frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m},\qquad p=\hbar k .
\end{aligned}\tag{3}\label{eq:dispersion}
$$

For equation $\eqref{eq:dispersion}$, the time derivative supplies $-i\omega$ and the second spatial derivative supplies $-k^2$. The simple plane wave is used here to check phase and signs. It is not a normalized wave packet.
